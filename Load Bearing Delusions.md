I can be an annoying person, there are many reasons why and I have worked long and hard on being less annoying over the years. One particular facet that has caused problems was a combination of two features:

1. Operating strongly on a world model, where the consistency of said world model dictates my ability to do almost anything. Thus creating a *strong drive for consistency*.
2. Innate curiosity about the minds of people around me and how they work

The first feature is what I credit most of my scientific ability to, the second my empathy. There are failure modes for both individually. 

Using a world model as the primary means for sorting information creates a very strong tension against the high entropy information that does not slot neatly into a world model.  This includes things such as names or arbitrary data bits that do not obey well structured patterns to be derived from. Studying biology was, in some ways, a masochistic exercise in extending my capacity. Forcing me to absorb large amounts of high entropy content and holding it in tension as I slowly collapsed it back down using chemistry and physics. This exercised my drive to form consistent patterns and is fundamental in my identity as a biophysicist, someone who can take information ranging from populations of organisms all the way down to a few biomolecules behaving in specific ways. Simultaneously it means that naming is a constant problem and long-term memorization is a skill I must constantly exercise for relatively little gain. 

Innate curiosity sounds good on paper, it's good to be curious about the people around you. It's a way to form connections, indeed most of my random connections in life are through this. A beautiful scarf, a novel method of doing a task, eyes tracking something I can't. Furthermore, this deepens connections with people, late nights spent trying to understand how a friend with aphantasia sorts through memories and ideas lead to enduring emotional intimacy. Unfortunately this instinct is like honey for narcissists and not all knowledge about other people is good to learn. 

When you combine these two things together you get a pressure that is exerted on other people, a desire to know and systemize them. Systemizing other people, I eventually learned, can be quite rude. Some people, including the aforementioned narcissists, thrive on being systemized and seen. They delight in the back and forth clarifying and re-clarifying their position. Indeed, great compliments are made from understanding how different aspects of a persons choices flow together to create an admirable outcome. But when you hit two pieces of information that do not gel, and you have a strong urge to unify them, I would recommend taking a few minutes first. Because, in all likelihood, you have come across a "load bearing delusion". 

A load bearing delusion is the case where someone has cognitive dissonance between core aspects of their identity "I am like peanuts" and "I dislike nuts" might be a weak case. These can be ironed out, such as modifying "I dislike nuts" into "I dislike most nuts". In this case the conversation flows easily. However, if the pillars carry real weight and impact on a persons life this gets messier. If the statements are "I am a politician who is a christian fundamentalist homophobe" and "I want to have sex with other men" then changing these aspects become harder. The former is enforced by their social contract in order to garner power, the later by some biological urge they have little control over (for if they did their former belief would have squashed it). This is what I mean by a **load bering delusion**, cognitive dissonance that if addressed would measurably damage some part of a persons life. 

Many cases of load bearing delusions are things that should be addressed and handled, even if it causes immediate suffering, for the long term gains. This does not mean all cases follow this format. Someone who is an atheist but believes their dead child is in heaven can be a case where the load bearing delusion is supporting their mental health. If removed, especially early in the healing process, it might cause a spiral and extremely bad life outcomes. 

Knowledge and consistency are not inherently good. This was a lesson that personally took me some time to come to terms with. Furthermore, it is also not my job or right to enforce consistency upon other people arbitrarily. If I am teaching or someone is operating within my sphere then yes, I will deal with any relevant inconsistencies. But when you collapse someones self-image you create an obligation to the world to help ensure it doesn't get worse. Some might say "oh we are all adults here, we are responsible for our own growth" to which I say "that's a miserable world". 

Load bearing delusions, by their nature, are antimemetic. If you recognize them they trigger some extremely negative feedback loops and stress, pushing you to turn attention away. When asking people "what are your cognitive dissonances or load bering delusions" you will never get a good answer, because if they knew them their own desire for self-consistency would gnaw on their mind. Or, alternatively, it creates a vulnerability that sharing makes far too dangerous. Contradictions.
